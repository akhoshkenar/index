PART I

01Statistical Data Science & Decision Analytics

From descriptive analytics to advanced inferential modeling, this area focused on turning raw operational, financial and service data into management insight, business clarity and measurable decisions.

125 projects
Friendly visual summary of dashboarding, forecasting, hypothesis testing and business decision support.
Representative higher-level cases

Three practical directions

Enterprise analytics and KPI dashboarding

Designed end-to-end analytics programs for private companies in banking, production, insurance and service operations, starting from data cleaning and exploratory analysis and ending with decision dashboards for managers. The work regularly revealed hidden cost drivers, customer patterns and process losses, with strongly positive decisions and better visibility for leadership. Across representative engagements, management reporting effort was reduced by roughly 20–40% and recurring reporting cycles were often shortened by 15–30%. These assignments typically combined SQL, Python, Excel, Power BI and statistical validation in one practical workflow.

  • Descriptive analytics
  • Visualization
  • Dashboards
  • KPI design

Hypothesis testing, DOE and process diagnostics

Implemented statistical studies for manufacturing and healthcare environments to test process assumptions, compare alternatives and identify influential factors behind quality and efficiency. In many cases, the work clarified where real variation was coming from and supported changes that delivered positive performance gains, better process stability and higher confidence in action plans. In representative process-improvement studies, defect or variation indicators improved by approximately 5–18%, while selected productivity or yield measures improved by around 4–12%. Typical methods included ANOVA, factorial DOE, response surface ideas and capability analysis.

  • ANOVA
  • DOE
  • SPC
  • Capability analysis

Forecasting, regression and causal business insight

Built demand forecasts, regression models and cause-oriented analyses for financial, healthcare and operational settings where planning accuracy mattered. These studies were often used to support staffing, supply, capacity and budgeting decisions, and they frequently produced positively improved planning confidence and clearer understanding of business drivers. For representative forecasting assignments, forecast error was reduced by about 8–22% against simple baselines, while planning accuracy commonly improved by roughly 5–12 percentage points. Time-series modeling, correlation screening, factor analysis and interpretable regression formed the core toolkit.

  • Forecasting
  • Regression
  • Factor analysis
  • Decision support
PART II

02Machine Learning & Deep Learning

This area translated business and research problems into predictive, classification and representation-learning solutions using both classical machine learning and modern deep-learning approaches.

69 projects
Cartoon summary of predictive modeling, clustering, computer vision and neural-network based learning.
Representative higher-level cases

Three practical directions

Predictive modeling for tabular business data

Developed classical machine-learning models for banking, healthcare, production and service datasets where the objective was to predict risk, demand, churn, defects or other critical outcomes. The work typically moved from feature engineering and model comparison to interpretation and deployment-ready reporting, and in many assignments it generated positive improvements in predictive accuracy and stronger decision support for managers. Against basic rule-based or linear baselines, representative models improved validation performance by approximately 5–15 percentage points, while false-positive or manual-review loads were reduced by around 10–25% in selected cases. Frequent methods included random forests, gradient boosting, logistic regression and XGBoost-family models.

  • Random forest
  • Boosting
  • Feature engineering
  • Model evaluation

Segmentation, anomaly detection and pattern discovery

Applied clustering, dimensionality reduction and unsupervised learning to uncover customer segments, operational behavior groups and unusual records in large datasets. These projects were particularly useful when organizations needed structure before action, and they often led to positive targeting strategies, sharper segmentation and earlier detection of abnormal patterns. In representative segmentation work, targeted groups improved response or prioritization efficiency by roughly 8–20%, while anomaly-screening workflows reduced the volume requiring manual review by approximately 15–35%. PCA, k-means, autoencoder-inspired ideas and distance-based diagnostics were used depending on the context.

  • Clustering
  • PCA
  • Anomaly detection
  • Exploration

Deep learning for image, sequential and advanced tasks

Designed deep-learning workflows for image-like, temporal or high-dimensional settings requiring stronger nonlinear learning capacity. These projects ranged from computer-vision style experiments to sequence modeling and transfer-learning setups, and they frequently produced positive gains in automation potential, richer representations and better task performance than baseline methods. In selected image and sequence tasks, performance improved by about 5–14 percentage points compared with simpler baselines, while automation reduced repetitive manual screening by roughly 20–50%. CNN, RNN, LSTM and transfer learning were among the main building blocks used.

  • CNN
  • LSTM / GRU
  • Transfer learning
  • PyTorch / TensorFlow
PART III

03Simulation & Digital Experimentation

Simulation projects were used to experiment safely, discover bottlenecks, compare policies and visualize dynamic behavior before implementation in real systems.

35 projects
Cartoon summary of discrete-event simulation, agent-based models and system-dynamics thinking.
Representative higher-level cases

Three practical directions

Discrete-event simulation for factories and operational flow

Implemented discrete-event simulation models for factories and process-intensive systems where throughput, queues, machine interaction and resource use mattered. These studies helped identify bottlenecks, test operational alternatives and communicate future-state options to management, with positive improvements commonly in the range of about 4–12% in key performance indicators. In several modeled scenarios, waiting or queue time was reduced by approximately 10–25% and utilization improved by around 5–10%. AnyLogic, Arena and SimPy were the main tools, and management teams were typically highly satisfied with the clarity of the findings.

  • AnyLogic
  • Arena
  • SimPy
  • Bottleneck analysis

Agent-based simulation for social networks and disease control

Designed agent-based models, sometimes combined with discrete-event logic, for academic and policy-oriented work related to social interaction, diffusion behavior and disease-control dynamics. These projects were useful for testing interventions and observing emergent effects that were difficult to study analytically, and they often provided positive insight into intervention design, network sensitivity and scenario understanding. In selected disease-control scenarios, alternative intervention policies produced modeled reductions of roughly 8–20% in peak exposure or spread-related indicators compared with baseline scenarios. The final outputs helped decision makers discuss mechanisms rather than isolated snapshots.

  • Agent-based modeling
  • Networks
  • Disease control
  • Scenario analysis

System-dynamics modeling for economic and social sectors

Built system-dynamics models in Vensim for economic, social and policy-oriented settings where feedback loops, delay structures and long-term behavior were central. These projects supported strategic discussions by converting complex interactions into interpretable causal structures, and they often produced positive improvements in strategic understanding and clearer dialogue about long-term consequences. Typical studies compared roughly 10–30 policy scenarios across 5–15 year horizons, allowing decision makers to see delayed effects and trade-offs that were not visible in static analysis. The work emphasized causal loops, stock-and-flow thinking and the communication of dynamic trade-offs.

  • Vensim
  • Feedback loops
  • Stock-flow models
  • Policy testing
PART IV

04Optimization, Supply Chain & Operations Research

These projects focused on turning operational complexity into mathematical models and practical planning tools for logistics, scheduling, inventory and network design.

90 projects
Cartoon summary of network design, routing, warehouse planning, schedules and mathematical optimization.
Representative higher-level cases

Three practical directions

Supply-chain network design and distribution planning

Formulated optimization studies for plant, warehouse and distribution decisions in settings where geography, cost and service levels had to be balanced at the same time. The resulting analyses were used to compare alternative network structures and transportation plans, often leading to positive reductions in logistics cost and stronger service-oriented planning decisions. Representative optimized scenarios reduced transportation or network cost by about 5–14% and total distance or handling effort by approximately 6–16%. Linear programming, mixed-integer programming and scenario analysis were the backbone of this work.

  • Network design
  • MIP
  • Transportation models
  • Scenario analysis

Inventory optimization and replenishment policy design

Designed inventory models covering EOQ, reorder point, safety stock, service-level planning and multi-echelon logic for production and distribution environments. These studies helped clients understand how to protect service without locking excessive capital in stock, and in many cases they created positive outcomes through better replenishment control, lower stock risk and healthier working-capital behavior. In representative cases, average inventory was reduced by around 8–20% while stockout or shortage exposure improved by approximately 10–30%. Analytical optimization was often combined with simulation checks to confirm practicality.

  • EOQ
  • ROP
  • Safety stock
  • Multi-echelon inventory

Scheduling, assignment and algorithmic decision support

Developed operations-research solutions for scheduling, assignment, resource use and planning problems in hospitals, production lines and service operations. The goal was usually to improve utilization, reduce delays and provide transparent decision rules, and the resulting models frequently delivered positive gains in resource efficiency and more structured day-to-day planning. Representative scheduling and assignment studies reduced waiting, delay or idle-time measures by roughly 7–22% and improved resource utilization by approximately 4–12%. Depending on the case, the work used exact optimization, heuristics or custom algorithm design.

  • Scheduling
  • Assignment
  • Heuristics
  • Resource planning
PART V

05Industrial, Healthcare & Management Consulting

Consulting work connected analysis with implementation by helping organizations redesign operations, improve process capability and translate evidence into management action.

100+ consultations
Cartoon summary of workshops, hospital improvement, production support, KPI design and executive guidance.
Representative higher-level cases

Four practical directions

Healthcare operations and patient-flow improvement

Supported hospital-oriented assignments around patient flow, capacity, queueing, readmission-oriented thinking and resource balancing. These engagements helped transform scattered operational concerns into clear action maps, and they commonly produced positive service-improvement discussions, better prioritization and stronger operational visibility for administrators. In representative flow-improvement cases, waiting-time measures decreased by around 8–20%, while selected resource-utilization indicators improved by approximately 5–12%. Analytical work was usually paired with practical process conversation and facility-level interpretation.

  • Patient flow
  • Capacity
  • Healthcare KPIs
  • Operational insight

Production-line improvement and quality systems

Worked with industrial teams on efficiency, waste reduction, variation control and quality improvement in production environments. By combining diagnostics, process mapping and improvement thinking, these studies often generated positive gains in throughput, clearer bottleneck understanding and stronger quality-control discipline. Representative production-improvement programs increased throughput by roughly 4–15% and reduced defect or rework indicators by approximately 5–20%. Lean, DMAIC, SPC and process capability methods were frequent supporting tools in this stream.

  • Lean
  • DMAIC
  • SPC
  • Process capability

Management dashboards and KPI architecture

Designed practical reporting structures so leaders could monitor strategic and operational health through a smaller, more meaningful set of indicators. This work often connected business objectives with measurable metrics and created positive improvements in reporting discipline, accountability and executive clarity. In representative dashboard programs, recurring reporting preparation time fell by roughly 25–50% and manual consolidation effort was reduced by approximately 30–60%. The outcome was usually a decision-oriented dashboard rather than a purely descriptive report.

  • KPI design
  • Balanced scorecard
  • Dashboards
  • Executive reporting

Cross-functional workshops and decision support

Facilitated workshops and advisory sessions bringing together technical, managerial and operational perspectives around shared problems. These settings were especially useful when organizations needed alignment before implementation, and they often resulted in positive consensus building, faster action planning and stronger cross-functional communication. Workshops typically narrowed 20–40 candidate issues into about 5–10 prioritized actions, helping teams move from broad discussion to a focused implementation agenda. The work combined structured analysis with communication and implementation framing.

  • Workshops
  • Decision support
  • Cross-functional alignment
  • Strategy
